# Transcription & Content Toolkit

A practical toolkit for turning audio and video into clear, usable written deliverables: transcripts, subtitles, captions, and voiceover-ready scripts.

> **Status:** Usable portfolio MVP with reusable templates, fictional demonstrations, and quality checklists. Sample content is illustrative and was not transcribed from real recordings.

## Start here

1. Review the [transcript style guide](templates/transcript-style-guide.md).
2. Use the [project intake questionnaire](templates/intake-questionnaire.md) to confirm scope and delivery requirements.
3. Use the [voiceover script template](templates/voiceover-script-template.md) for narration projects.
4. Review the [multi-speaker transcript example](examples/sample-transcript-multi-speaker.md).
5. Compare [verbatim vs. clean transcript editing](examples/before-after-edit.md).
6. Inspect the [SRT](examples/sample-captions.srt) and [WebVTT](examples/sample-captions.vtt) caption examples.
7. Run the [quality-control checklist](checklists/quality-control.md) and [delivery checklist](checklists/delivery-checklist.md).

## Services this toolkit supports

- Verbatim or clean transcription
- Speaker labels and timestamps
- SRT subtitles and WebVTT captions
- Proofreading and transcript formatting
- Voiceover / narration script formatting
- Clear delivery packaging and quality checks

## Quality principles

- Accuracy over polish: do not invent unclear words.
- Mark uncertainty transparently.
- Verify names, numbers, terminology, speaker labels, and timestamps against the media.
- Keep verbatim, clean transcript, and summary deliverables distinct.
- Follow client permissions and privacy requirements.
- Confirm caption timing and platform requirements against the actual source.

## Repository structure

- `templates/` — style guide, intake questionnaire, voiceover script
- `examples/` — fictional transcript, before/after editing, SRT and WebVTT samples
- `checklists/` — quality-control and final delivery checks

## Current limitations

Fictional examples demonstrate formatting only; they do not establish transcription accuracy on real media. In client work, timestamps, speaker attribution, caption synchronization, and uncertain speech must be reviewed against the source recording. Automated speech recognition can be used as a draft when appropriate, but must be checked.
