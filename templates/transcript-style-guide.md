# Transcript Style Guide

Use this guide as a default. Follow client or platform requirements when they differ.

## Before starting
- Confirm language, dialect, verbatim level, speaker count, timestamp interval, and output format.
- Ask how to handle filler words, false starts, crosstalk, and non-speech sounds.
- Confirm whether sensitive details should be redacted.

## Speaker labels and timestamps
- Label speakers consistently: `Speaker 1:`, `Speaker 2:` until names are confirmed.
- Use real speaker names only when supplied or reliably identified.
- Use `[00:00:12]` timestamps when requested; never estimate a timestamp and present it as verified.
- Keep timestamps consistent throughout the document.

## Verbatim levels
- **Strict verbatim:** retain filler words, repetitions, false starts, and meaningful non-speech sounds.
- **Clean verbatim:** remove distracting filler only when it does not change meaning; preserve the speaker's intent.
- **Edited transcript:** improve grammar and readability with permission; do not present it as verbatim.

## Unclear audio
- Use `[inaudible 00:01:24]` when speech cannot be recovered.
- Use `[unclear: possible “...”]` only when a plausible reading is useful and uncertainty is explicit.
- Do not guess names, numbers, or technical terms. Recheck the audio and surrounding context.

## Non-speech sounds
Include meaningful sounds when relevant, such as `[laughter]`, `[applause]`, or `[door closes]`. Avoid cluttering a transcript with irrelevant background noise unless requested.

## Final review
- Verify names, dates, numbers, acronyms, and specialist terminology.
- Check speaker attribution and timestamp placement.
- Preserve meaning, tone, and the requested verbatim level.
- Mark any unresolved issues for the client.
